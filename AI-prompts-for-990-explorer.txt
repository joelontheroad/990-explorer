Here's info on PACs. There are two categories:

CATEGORY 1

527s Not Reporting to the FEC ("Soft Money" 527s)
These file with the IRS and are publicly searchable:

Form	Purpose
Form 8871	Notice of 527 status (registration)
Form 8872	Periodic report of contributions and expenditures — this is the closest analog to Form 990
Form 1120-POL	Annual income tax return (if taxable income > $100)

Form 8872 requires disclosure of contributions of $200+ (donor name, address, occupation, employer, amount, date) and expenditures of $500+ (recipient name, address, occupation, employer, amount, date, purpose).  Filing frequency is quarterly or monthly depending on the organization's chosen schedule. 

Where to find them: The IRS maintains a public search tool at irs.gov/charities-non-profits/political-organizations/political-organization-filing-and-disclosure. Electronic filings go back to 2020; paper filings go back to January 2012. 

ProPublica's 527 Explorer (projects.propublica.org/527-explorer) repackages the IRS data into a much more user-friendly interface with searchable donor and expenditure breakdowns.

CATEGORY 2
527s That Are Also FEC Political Committees (Super PACs, Traditional PACs)
These file with the FEC instead of the IRS (they're exempt from Forms 8871/8872). Their reports are publicly available at fec.gov/data, searchable by committee name, candidate, amount, and report type. FEC filings are generally considered more granular and timely (including 48-hour reports in the final stretch of elections). 

Key Differences from Form 990
Form 8872 is focused on contributions and expenditures with individual-level detail (who gave, who received), not a full balance sheet/income statement like Form 990.
There's no equivalent to Form 990's "Statement of Functional Expenses" or program service lines.
The Form 1120-POL (annual return) is closer to a 990-T in scope — it primarily reports taxable income, not full financials.
So the transparency is there, but it's structured differently: more transaction-level detail on money flows, less on overall financial health.
